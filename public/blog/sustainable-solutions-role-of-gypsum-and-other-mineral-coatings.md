## How Does Gypsum Remediate Sodic and Alkaline Agricultural Soils?

Gypsum (Calcium Sulfate Dihydrate, CaSO₄·2H₂O) is the premier mineral amendment for reclaiming sodic and alkaline soils (pH Above 8.2, Exchangeable Sodium Percentage ESP Above 15%). When applied to sodic soils, gypsum provides high-affinity divalent Calcium cations (Ca²⁺) that chemically displace toxic monovalent Sodium ions (Na⁺) from clay colloidal exchange sites. The displaced sodium forms soluble sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄), which leaches below the root zone with irrigation water, restoring soil flocculation, porosity, and hydraulic conductivity.

## Understanding Soil Salinity, Sodicity, and Alkalinity

Managing problem soils requires precise chemical diagnosis:

1. **Saline Soils (High Soluble Salts):** Characterized by high Electrical Conductivity (EC_e Above 4.0 dS/m), neutral to slightly alkaline pH (Below 8.5), and an Exchangeable Sodium Percentage below 15%. Excessive soluble salts draw water away from root cells via osmotic suction.
2. **Sodic Soils (High Exchangeable Sodium):** Characterized by high ESP (Above 15%), high pH (often 8.5 to 10.0), and low EC. Sodium ions destroy soil structure by dispersing clay platelets into an impermeable, waterlogged slurry that bakes into hard rock upon drying.
3. **Saline-Sodic Soils:** Contain both excessive soluble salts (EC_e Above 4.0 dS/m) and high exchangeable sodium (ESP Above 15%).

```
Cation Displacement Reaction on Clay Colloid:
2 Na⁺-Clay (Dispersed Impermeable Colloid) + CaSO₄·2H₂O (Gypsum)
  → Ca²⁺-Clay (Flocculated Porous Aggregate) + Na₂SO₄ (Leachable Soluble Salt) + 2 H₂O
```

## The 4 Steps of Gypsum-Driven Soil Reclamation

### Step 1: Chemical Calcium Exchange
The divalent calcium ion (Ca²⁺) has a significantly higher charge density and ionic affinity for negative clay surfaces than monovalent sodium (Na⁺) or magnesium (Mg²⁺). Gypsum dissolves moderately (2.5 g/L in water), providing a sustained electrolyte stream of Ca²⁺ ions to replace Na⁺ on exchange sites.

### Step 2: Clay Flocculation and Aggregation
As calcium replaces sodium, the repulsive electrical double layer between clay platelets compresses. Clay particles flocculate into stable, water-resistant crumb macro-aggregates, immediately expanding macro-pore volume.

### Step 3: Leaching of Displaced Sodium
Irrigation or monsoon rainfall leaches the liberated sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄) deep below the active root zone.

### Step 4: Normalizing Soil pH and Nutrient Availability
Displacing sodium bicarbonates (NaHCO₃) reduces soil pH from toxic levels (Above 9.0) back into the agronomically optimal range (6.5 to 7.8), unlocking immobilized phosphorus (P₂O₅), iron (Fe²⁺), and zinc (Zn²⁺).

## Soil Diagnostic Parameters & Gypsum Requirement (GR)

| Soil Classification | Soil pH | Electrical Conductivity (EC_e) | Exchangeable Sodium % (ESP) | Primary Chemical Amendment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal Healthy Soil** | 6.5 – 7.8 | Below 2.0 dS/m | Below 10% | Maintenance organic nutrition (PROM / PDM) |
| **Saline Soil** | Below 8.5 | Above 4.0 dS/m | Below 15% | Good-quality water leaching + Organic amendments |
| **Sodic / Alkaline Soil** | 8.5 – 10.5 | Below 4.0 dS/m | Above 15% | **Granular Mineral Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O)** |
| **Saline-Sodic Soil** | Variable (Above 8.2) | Above 4.0 dS/m | Above 15% | Gypsum application followed by deep drainage leaching |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much gypsum is required per acre to reclaim sodic soils?
For moderately sodic soils (ESP 15% to 25%), apply 500 kg to 1,000 kg of agricultural gypsum per acre incorporated into the top 15 cm of soil before irrigation. For severe sodicity (ESP Above 30%), university soil testing should determine the exact Gypsum Requirement (GR).

### Can gypsum be applied to neutral or acidic soils?
Yes. In non-sodic soils, gypsum serves as a neutral, non-pH-altering source of soluble Calcium (18% to 21% Ca) and Sulfate-Sulfur (14% to 16% S), highly beneficial for sulfur-demanding crops like groundnut, mustard, and onion.
